How to get your local business found in AI search

More people than ever ask ChatGPT and Google AI to recommend a local business. Here's what actually gets you named — in plain English, from someone who runs local businesses too.

In 2026, 45% of consumers used AI assistants like ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity to find local businesses — up from just 6% a year earlier (BrightLocal 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey). Yet ChatGPT was measured naming only about 1.2% of local businesses. Nearly half of customers are now asking AI for a recommendation, and almost no local business shows up in the answer. These tools don't show ten links. They name one or two businesses and explain why. Being named is the new word-of-mouth referral.

The short answer

AI tools recommend the businesses they can understand and verify with confidence. That comes down to four things you can control:

  • A complete Google Business Profile — filled out fully, kept current, with photos and correct hours.
  • Consistent information everywhere — the same business name, address, and phone across every site. Mismatches confuse AI.
  • Strong, recent reviews — the single biggest factor. Fresh reviews from real customers, gathered honestly.
  • Clear, answer-first content — a page that plainly states what you do, where you serve, and what makes you different.

Why "answer-first" content matters

AI tools pull short, self-contained answers straight from web pages. If your page clearly answers the questions customers actually ask — phrased the way they'd say them — the AI can lift your answer and name you. Generic "we do it all" pages give the AI nothing specific to grab, so it picks a competitor who was clearer. Specific beats generic, every time.

There's a second half to this: broad searches and specific searches are different games. Ask AI for "the best house cleaner in town" and it names whoever has the most reviews — usually the biggest companies, and a smaller business can't easily win that. But ask for something specific — "vacation rental cleaning," "cabinet refinishing," "drywall repair" — and the business that clearly specializes in exactly that can get named first, even over competitors with far more reviews. The catch: you have to genuinely be the specialist and say so plainly. Don't fight for the broad term you can't win. Own the specific thing you're great at.

The honest truth about the timeline

This isn't an overnight switch. Fixing your profile and consistency can help within weeks. Reviews and content authority build over months. The businesses that win treat visibility as a steady habit — not a one-time fix. Consistency is the whole game.

Common questions

Why isn't my business showing up on Google or AI search?

Usually because your information is incomplete, inconsistent across the web, or thin on reviews. If your Google profile is missing details, your name or phone differs between sites, or you have few recent reviews, AI struggles to recommend you confidently. Fixing consistency and steadily gathering real reviews is the common path to visibility.

Do I need a website to get found?

It helps but isn't strictly required. What matters most is a complete Google Business Profile, consistent information across the web, and strong reviews. One well-structured page stating who you are, what you do, and where can work as your web presence and give AI something clear to cite.

Can I actually see if AI is sending me customers?

Yes. If you have Google Analytics on your site, go to Reports → Acquisition → Traffic acquisition and look at your traffic sources. When someone reaches you from ChatGPT, it shows up as "chatgpt.com / ai-assistant" (Google labels the channel "AI Assistant"). It's a real, checkable way to confirm AI is referring customers to you — and most business owners don't even know to look for it.

What's the single most important factor?

Reviews. A steady flow of real, recent Google reviews is a top factor in both Google rankings and AI recommendations. Between two similar businesses, AI tends to name the one with more and fresher reviews. Gather them honestly, ask every customer, never gate or incentivize.
