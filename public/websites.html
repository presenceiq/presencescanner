Common questions

How much should a small business website cost?

Search that question and you get agency guides quoting $500 to $15,000, or $500 to $25,000, and every one of them ends with "it depends." That range is real, because it covers everything from a one-page site to a full online shop. What it does not do is answer your question. Here is a number instead. A one-page small business website, built by hand, is $299 with me. One payment, and that includes me buying your web address for the first year and setting it up in your name. Extra pages are $99 each. Keeping a one-page site online costs nothing. If you need an online shop, a booking system or twenty pages of content, the agency ranges are honest and you should budget accordingly. If you need one good page that Google and AI search can actually read, you do not need to spend thousands.

What does $299 actually get me?

Your web address, bought and set up in your name. One page, built by hand. Your services written out specifically, the towns you cover, your real customer reviews as text, a button that dials your phone, and a set of questions and answers customers actually ask. Underneath that, the work nobody sees: the labels that tell Google what kind of business you are, a file that invites the AI tools in rather than shutting them out, and your page submitted to Google and Bing by me so it gets found instead of sitting there.

Why one page and not five?

Because one page carries the heavy work. Everything that makes you findable, your services, your reviews, your questions and answers and the work underneath, all sits on that page and all of it counts. My own view, from building these, is that five thin pages do less for you than one good one. Start with the one, live with it a while, and add pages later if you find you actually need them. They are $99 each and they are not going anywhere.

Who owns the website?

You do. I buy the web address, but the account is created in your name with your email and your phone number, and you get the password. The account the site runs on is in your name too, and you invite me in to do the work. You can remove me with two clicks whenever you like, and the files are yours on request. It is yours, not rented from me.

No. You pay the $299 once, and the first year of your web address is in that. How long does it take?

A few days, usually. The build itself takes under an hour. What takes the time is you and I talking through what goes on it, and then waiting for your web address to connect.

What if I do not like it?

You get one round of changes included. Tell me what is wrong and I fix it. Typos and wrong phone numbers are always free to fix, whenever you spot them.

I already have a Facebook page. Is that not enough?

Facebook is somebody else's property and it is hard for Google and the AI tools to read. When someone asks ChatGPT for a painter in North Port, it draws on web pages it can read, not on your Facebook posts. A page of your own, at your own web address, is something you control and something those tools can quote.

Do I have to be on the PresenceScanner directory?

No. They are separate things. The directory listing is free and always will be. This is paid work on a website of your own.

What makes this different from Wix or Squarespace?

Those hand you a template and you do the work. Here you answer a few questions and I do the rest. And the part most template sites skip is the part that matters now: the labels Google reads, questions and answers written out where people can see them, and the page submitted properly so Google and the AI tools can find it.
